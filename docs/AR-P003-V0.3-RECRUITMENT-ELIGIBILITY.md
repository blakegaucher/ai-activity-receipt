# AR-P003 v0.3 — Recruitment Eligibility and Screening Rule

> **Status:** Reviewer-population and eligibility design selected; recruitment instrument remains a development draft.
> **Hard stop:** No reviewer recruitment or human execution is authorized.

This document translates the selected reviewer-population decision into a future screening rule. It does not collect participant information and is not a recruitment notice.

## Eligibility rule

A future reviewer is eligible only if all of the following are true:

1. The person has at least 1 year of relevant professional or practical experience in one or more eligible domains.
2. The person confirms sufficient English proficiency to understand the technical evidence, instructions, and structured response interface.
3. None of the prespecified conflict/exposure exclusions applies.
4. The person agrees to the study-case external-assistance rule.

Eligible domains are:

- technical audit;
- cybersecurity;
- compliance;
- AI governance;
- incident investigation or incident review;
- software or system operations;
- technical assurance;
- closely related evidence-review work.

A degree, certification, or particular job title is not required if the relevant practical-experience criterion is met.

## Fields to record

The future screener must record, at minimum:

- eligible domain or domains;
- one total relevant-experience band: 1–2 years, 3–5 years, or 6+ years;
- confirmation of sufficient English proficiency;
- whether the person has prior general familiarity with AI Activity Receipt;
- a pass/fail response for each exclusion below;
- agreement to the study-case external-assistance rule;
- final eligibility status and the version of this rule used.

No professional-domain quota, reviewer count, case workload, allocation, or stopping rule is selected by this document.

## Exclusion screen

Exclude a person if they:

- constructed or materially edited the sealed evaluation cases;
- created or had access to hidden gold answers;
- participated in scoring-rule development using the sealed cases;
- scored the confirmatory responses;
- accessed protected confirmatory analysis material before completing their assigned cases.

## External-assistance agreement

During study cases, reviewers may not use external web search, external AI assistants, another person, or outside tools/evidence not supplied by the study. An exception requires an explicitly versioned protocol amendment made before confirmatory execution.

## Why this population is methodologically relevant

The selected professional-review population is intended to improve ecological validity for the audit task, not to imply that professional experience guarantees better performance. Human-centered AI research shows that domain familiarity, practical experience, and expertise can materially change how people interpret and rely on AI explanations. For example, Zhao, Castelle, and Turkay (2025; DOI 10.1145/3757583) report different explanation-use patterns among practitioners with different combinations of practical experience and domain expertise. Related XAI user studies also report changes in interpretation, response time, and perceived helpfulness when users work inside versus outside familiar domains.

For AR-P003, this supports recording relevant experience and constraining inference to the population actually recruited. It does not justify assuming a performance advantage in advance, excluding eligible reviewers merely for lacking a particular title or credential, or treating experience band as a confirmatory outcome.

## Recruitment and reporting boundary

Recruitment channels, professional-domain composition, and final allocation remain to be determined under the still-unfrozen study protocol. Any later claim must describe the population actually recruited and must not generalize a convenience or unrepresentative sample to all auditors, cybersecurity professionals, compliance professionals, AI-governance professionals, or a broader professional population.

Actual contact information, screening responses, consent records, and reviewer data are not public-repository artifacts and must remain private unless separately authorized under applicable privacy, ethics, release, and licensing decisions.
