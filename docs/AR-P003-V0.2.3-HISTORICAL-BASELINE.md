## C1 auxiliary AI reviewer result

The frozen v0.2.3 corpus contained 80 synthetic episodes.

C1, an auxiliary AI reviewer, completed all 80:

- control raw logs: **40 / 40**;
- Receipt + raw logs: **40 / 40**;
- every frozen non-timing endpoint: **100% in both conditions**;
- authorization F1 across all 80: **1.00**;
- timing: **unavailable** for all 80;
- median confidence: **95.0 control vs 96.0 Receipt**;
- mean confidence: **94.53 control vs 96.47 Receipt**.

The correct interpretation is narrow:

- the v0.2.3 response contract was consistently interpretable to this auxiliary AI reviewer across the eight synthetic episode classes;
- the result did **not** demonstrate a Receipt accuracy advantage because the control condition was also at ceiling;
- the result did **not** demonstrate a speed advantage because reviewer wall-clock time was not measured;
- the result did **not** establish a human auditing benefit.

No protocol, corpus, assignment, scoring semantics, endpoint definitions, denominators, exclusions, or scoring rules should be changed retroactively after this result.

## Post-C1 methodology audit

A subsequent static audit identified reasons not to use the frozen v0.2.3 human arm as the sole basis for a serious human-benefit or productivity claim.

Key findings included:

1. **Ceiling / label leakage** — all 60 non-`none` incident episodes exposed the exact incident label verbatim in raw logs.
2. **Presentation asymmetry** — the pretty-printed Receipt condition was about 59.9% longer on average because it included the raw logs plus the Receipt.
3. **Fixed class order** — cases were grouped by episode class, creating learning/order risk.
4. **Timing mismatch** — the runner did not implement the pause/visibility behavior described by the frozen timing plan.
5. **No safe breaks** — the next case timer began immediately after submission.
6. **JSON-entry burden** — manual JSON editing contaminated any human timing endpoint with formatting/syntax effort.
7. **No bad-Receipt robustness** — the corpus did not test stale, incomplete, or conflicting Receipts.
8. **Limited human arm** — after the auxiliary C1 arm, only three human reviewer IDs remained in that frozen assignment design.

These are historical findings, not a reason to rewrite the frozen benchmark.

## Decision carried forward

The frozen v0.2.3 artifact remains preserved as an auxiliary-AI interpretability baseline.

The stronger next evaluation path is **AR-P003 v0.3**, built as a fresh human-centered benchmark with:

- a fresh sealed corpus;
- randomized/balanced reviewer assignment;
- lower answer leakage;
- reliable timing instrumentation;
- explicit stale/incomplete/conflicting Receipt challenge cases;
- frozen endpoints, exclusions, analysis rules, and hashes before scored human data are inspected.

The current v0.3 materials in this repository are still **draft/development artifacts**. They are not a completed human experiment.

## Architecture decision preserved from the post-C1 review

The Activity Receipt is a derived audit view rather than the authoritative record:

```text
raw evidence / traces
        |
        v
Canonical Activity Record
        |
        v
Activity Receipt View
```

The canonical record carries the machine-oriented evidence structure. The compact Receipt remains traceable back to that record through its integrity binding.

Private chain-of-thought remains outside the record and Receipt.

## Claim boundary

The historical v0.2.3 result must not be described as evidence of:

- improved human audit accuracy;
- faster auditing;
- productivity improvement;
- real-world safety improvement;
- legal/regulatory compliance;
- standards conformance;
- commercial effectiveness.

It is historical synthetic/technical evidence and should remain labeled that way.
