# AR-P003 v0.3 — Human-Centered Audit Reconstruction Protocol

> **Status:** Candidate preregistration draft — **not frozen and not yet executed**.  
> **Version:** v0.3-draft-2026-09-18  
> **Evidence boundary:** This protocol defines a future human-centered evaluation. It does not itself establish that AI Activity Receipt improves audit accuracy, speed, safety, compliance, or productivity.

## 1. Research question

For the same underlying AI-agent episode, does providing an **AI Activity Receipt in addition to the same source evidence** change how accurately, reliably, or quickly an independent human reviewer can reconstruct consequential activity?

The evaluation compares two presentation conditions:

1. **Control** — heterogeneous underlying evidence only.
2. **Receipt** — the same underlying evidence plus a standardized Activity Receipt.

For ordinary cases, the Receipt must satisfy **evidence symmetry**: every material fact in the Receipt must be derivable from the source evidence supplied in the corresponding control condition.

## 2. Primary reconstruction targets

Reviewers reconstruct only observable/auditable facts. They are not asked for private chain-of-thought.

The scored targets are:

- material actions;
- authorization violation state;
- material source attribution;
- incident classification;
- verification state;
- missing-evidence identification;
- time to final submitted reconstruction.

Reviewer confidence is collected as a secondary descriptive measure.

## 3. Case strata

The sealed evaluation corpus should include prespecified strata.

### Ordinary evidence-symmetric cases

The Receipt is a faithful derived representation of the same evidence available in control.

These cases are the primary basis for estimating whether the representation changes reconstruction performance.

### Challenge cases

Challenge cases test whether reviewers notice a Receipt that should **not** be trusted at face value.

Prespecified challenge types:

- **stale Receipt** — valid at an earlier record state but no longer current;
- **incomplete Receipt** — omits material evidence present in the source record;
- **conflicting Receipt** — contains a material claim inconsistent with source evidence.

Challenge cases must be labeled in the hidden case specification before reviewer data are inspected. Their results are reported separately from ordinary cases.

## 4. Corpus construction

The confirmatory corpus must be **fresh and sealed** relative to development work.

Requirements:

- do not reuse AR-P003 v0.2.x scored cases as confirmatory v0.3 cases;
- do not use the repository smoke fixtures as evaluation cases;
- use heterogeneous evidence formats rather than one repeated template;
- avoid answer-revealing filenames, incident labels, headings, or field names in control materials;
- include sufficient ambiguity and difficulty to avoid ceiling effects;
- preserve null, confusing, failed, and hard cases after unsealing;
- maintain a hidden case specification containing the gold reconstruction and stratum;
- assign stable case IDs that do not encode the correct answer.

Development cases may be used to debug instructions and tooling, but they must be marked **development-only** and excluded from confirmatory analysis.

## 5. Reviewer assignment and randomization

Use a randomized balanced incomplete-block design.

- Each reviewer receives cases from both presentation conditions.
- A reviewer must **never see the same underlying case in both conditions**.
- Across reviewers, each case should be represented in both conditions as evenly as mathematically possible.
- Each reviewer should receive control and Receipt cases as evenly as mathematically possible.
- The current development generator guarantees per-case and per-reviewer condition-count imbalance of at most one by using a deterministic balanced bipartite edge-coloring procedure after reviewer/case incidence is selected.
- Case order is independently randomized per reviewer.
- Challenge cases are distributed according to a prespecified assignment rule, and aggregate condition counts by challenge stratum must be inspected before freeze.
- The assignment seed, generator version, balance diagnostics, and final assignment file are frozen before confirmatory responses are inspected.
- Reviewer bundles and response exports should carry the exact assignment version and SHA-256 binding, and the analysis merge must verify reviewer/case/order/condition against that frozen assignment rather than trusting a response-side condition label.

This design reduces between-reviewer variability while avoiding direct memory/carryover from showing the same episode twice to one reviewer.

The earlier development allocator assigned conditions greedily after selecting cases. Testing showed that it could preserve per-case balance yet still produce reviewer-level 4/2 condition splits for six-case workloads. The current generator replaces that logic before any confirmatory assignment has been frozen or used for human data collection.

The presentation condition cannot be fully blinded because a Receipt is visibly present. Reviewer-facing instructions should therefore describe the task neutrally and should not tell participants that the expected result is a Receipt advantage.

## 6. Reviewer eligibility

Before freezing recruitment criteria, define the intended target population.

At minimum, record:

- relevant technical/audit experience bands;
- minimum language proficiency needed to understand the evidence;
- whether prior familiarity with the project is allowed;
- exclusion criteria for anyone who helped construct or score the sealed cases.

If the intended claim concerns a particular professional population, recruitment and reporting should match that population rather than generalizing from a convenience sample.

## 7. Human-participant safeguards

Before recruitment, obtain whatever ethics/REB/IRB review, exemption, or other determination is applicable to the actual research setting.

The study should:

- provide informed study information/consent where applicable;
- collect only data needed for the evaluation;
- use pseudonymous reviewer IDs in analysis files;
- avoid collecting private chain-of-thought;
- avoid unnecessary sensitive personal information;
- document retention/access rules;
- allow withdrawal as required by the applicable study process.

These safeguards are protocol requirements, not a claim that a particular regulatory regime applies to every future pilot.

## 8. Reviewer task

For each case, the reviewer submits a structured final reconstruction containing:

- selected material-action IDs or canonical action labels;
- whether an authorization violation occurred;
- selected material-source IDs;
- incident labels;
- verification state;
- whether important evidence is missing or insufficient;
- confidence on a fixed ordinal scale;
- final submit time captured by the study system.

Free-text notes may be collected for qualitative debugging, but they are not a substitute for the prespecified scored fields.

## 9. Timing

Timing must be instrumented by the study system rather than estimated by the reviewer.

Record at minimum:

- case presentation timestamp;
- final submission timestamp;
- explicit pause/break events if the interface permits pausing;
- technical interruption flags.

Primary time-to-reconstruction is elapsed active wall-clock time under the prespecified timer rules.

Do not remove slow valid responses simply because they are inconvenient. Timing exclusions must be limited to prespecified technical/data-quality conditions.

If reliable timing is unavailable, **no speed or time-saving claim may be made**.

### Current development runner implementation

A development-only offline runner now records both wall and active elapsed time, explicit manual pause events, browser hidden/visible events, technical-issue flags, and a safe intermission between cases. The next case timer begins only after the reviewer explicitly starts it.

The runner uses structured response controls rather than raw JSON entry and keeps hidden gold labels/strata outside the reviewer-facing browser bundle.

This implementation is not yet the frozen study instrument. Browser/device compatibility, pause semantics, the primary timing field, response vocabularies, and technical-exclusion rules must be tested and frozen before confirmatory execution.

See [AR-P003 v0.3 Development Offline Runner](AR-P003-V0.3-OFFLINE-RUNNER.md).

## 10. Scored endpoints

No post-hoc weighted composite score is the primary endpoint.

Report the prespecified components separately.

### Set-valued reconstruction endpoints

For material actions, material sources, and incidents, compute set precision, recall, and F1.

For an empty gold set and empty predicted set, define F1 = 1.0.

### Categorical endpoints

Report exact accuracy for:

- authorization-violation state;
- verification state;
- missing-evidence state.

### Timing endpoint

Report time to final reconstruction using all valid timed responses under the frozen timing rules.

### Secondary descriptive endpoints

- reviewer confidence;
- reviewer agreement;
- false incident flags;
- omission patterns;
- challenge-case detection patterns.

## 11. Statistical analysis plan

The confirmatory analysis plan must be finalized before unsealing scored outcomes.

Minimum reporting:

- condition-specific means/proportions for each endpoint;
- absolute condition differences;
- uncertainty intervals;
- reviewer-level and case-level counts;
- ordinary cases separately from challenge cases;
- sensitivity results for any prespecified technical exclusions.

Because observations are crossed by reviewer and case, analysis should account for both sources of variation. A mixed-effects or crossed-effects model is appropriate when assumptions and sample size support it; otherwise use a prespecified resampling procedure that respects reviewer/case clustering.

Do not choose the model after seeing which analysis gives the most favorable result.

## 12. Sample size and power

The confirmatory sample size is **not yet frozen**.

A development-only screening utility is published at `benchmark/arp003_v0_3/plan_sample_size.py`, with illustrative scenarios in `planning-scenarios.example.json`. It explores independent-observation approximations.

A second development-only utility, `benchmark/arp003_v0_3/plan_crossed_design.py`, now uses the actual balanced reviewer/case assignment, explicit reviewer and case random effects, and two-way cluster-robust covariance for binary-endpoint sensitivity analysis. Its example scenarios center one sensitivity grid on the earlier 108-reviewer / 72-case / 24-cases-per-reviewer planning proposal.

The crossed simulator addresses design structure, but its baseline performance, effect, and variance assumptions are still illustrative and must not be treated as a frozen power calculation.

Before confirmatory data collection:

1. choose the primary endpoint(s) and minimally meaningful effect/precision target;
2. estimate plausible variability using development-only data or defensible external assumptions;
3. perform and record a power or precision analysis;
4. estimate reviewer-level and case-level dependence or choose a defensible prespecified adjustment/simulation strategy;
5. freeze the reviewer count, case count, allocation, and stopping rule.

Do not repeatedly inspect confirmatory outcomes and stop when a favorable result appears.

See `docs/AR-P003-V0.3-PLANNING.md` and `docs/AR-P003-V0.3-CROSSED-PLANNING.md` for the current development sensitivity analyses and their limitations.

## 13. Missing data and exclusions

Prespecify the handling of:

- incomplete reviewer submissions;
- technical timer failures;
- corrupted case presentation;
- duplicate submissions;
- reviewer withdrawal;
- cases later shown to have an invalid gold specification.

A difficult, slow, incorrect, or unfavorable result is **not** itself an exclusion criterion.

Any post-unsealing defect discovered in a case remains documented. If a defect makes a prespecified endpoint uninterpretable, report the defect and the rule used to handle it rather than silently deleting the case.

## 14. Claim gates

The project must separate measured results from broader claims.

### Accuracy/reliability claim gate

Do not claim that Receipts improve human audit reconstruction unless the relevant prespecified human endpoint(s), uncertainty analysis, and direction/effect criteria support that statement.

### Speed claim gate

Do not claim faster auditing or productivity benefit unless reliable timing was collected and the prespecified timing analysis supports it.

### Safety/compliance claim gate

AR-P003 does not by itself establish real-world safety improvement, legal compliance, regulatory compliance, or standards conformance.

### Challenge-case claim gate

A Receipt advantage on ordinary cases must not be presented without also reporting whether reviewers were misled by stale, incomplete, or conflicting Receipts.

## 15. Freeze procedure

Before confirmatory reviewers begin:

- finalize this protocol;
- finalize reviewer instructions;
- finalize case corpus and hidden gold labels;
- run and archive the case-package lint/leakage report for the frozen corpus;
- finalize assignment/randomization files;
- finalize scoring code and dependency versions;
- finalize exclusion rules;
- finalize statistical analysis plan;
- compute and record SHA-256 hashes for all frozen artifacts;
- record a freeze timestamp and protocol version;
- restrict access to hidden gold/corpus materials as appropriate.

After freeze, any material protocol, corpus, scorer, or endpoint change creates a new evaluation version. Do not silently modify v0.3 and treat the same data as independent confirmation.

## 16. Reproducibility package after evaluation

After appropriate de-identification and any necessary holdback period, publish as much as can responsibly be released:

- frozen protocol;
- reviewer instructions;
- scoring code;
- assignment-generation code;
- analysis code;
- case materials or a releasable derivative;
- gold labels where release will not invalidate planned follow-on testing;
- exclusions and defects;
- null and negative results alongside positive results.

## 17. Development tooling and smoke tests

The repository now includes development utilities for component-level scoring, reviewer/case assignment, response-record validation, case-package linting, and SHA-256 freeze manifests.

### Case-package development contract

Before a candidate corpus is frozen, each development case package should encode the comparison as:

- **control:** the declared underlying reviewer evidence;
- **Receipt:** that same declared reviewer evidence plus the Receipt.

Analysis-only files, including gold labels, must remain outside the reviewer-facing file set.

The development linter also checks relative-path safety, file existence, Receipt-state/stratum consistency, exact prespecified forbidden leakage markers, and produces file hashes for review. Passing this linter is necessary but not sufficient: it does not prove that the case is realistic, that gold labels are correct, or that all semantic leakage has been removed.

Repository code may include synthetic smoke records solely to verify that these utilities behave deterministically.

Those records:

- are not human data;
- are not part of the sealed AR-P003 corpus;
- cannot support an effectiveness claim;
- may be inspected and changed during development.

## 18. External research alignment

This protocol direction is informed by current public evaluation/governance guidance, including:

- NIST AI RMF measurement guidance on documenting metrics/test sets and evaluating systems under relevant conditions;
- NIST human-centered AI measurement work;
- NIST work on blind/sequestered evaluation to reduce contamination;
- the 2026 NIST TEVV-Athlon draft direction for structured, customizable evaluation of AI systems, including agentic systems.

These references inform methodology only; they do not imply NIST review, endorsement, or adoption of AI Activity Receipt.
